01 — Who they are

King Gnu formed in Tokyo in 2013, first as a duo called Mrs. Vinci, then as the five-piece Srv.Vinci, before settling into its current lineup and renaming itself King Gnu in 2017 — after the wildebeest, an animal that gathers into enormous mixed herds.

Songwriter and guitarist Daiki Tsuneta and keyboardist/vocalist Satoru Iguchi trade the lead vocal mid-song, sometimes mid-phrase, backed by bassist Kazuki Arai and drummer Yu Seki. The band calls its own genre "Tokyo New Mixture Style" — rock built on a foundation of jazz theory and classical composition, with hip-hop and R&B stitched through it.

Their 2019 single "Hakujitsu" (白日, "Broad Daylight") — written as the theme for the drama Innocence: False Accusation Lawyer — turned them from a cult act into one of Japan's biggest live draws, and 2020's CEREMONY topped the Oricon albums chart.

03 — A few facts

01

Two frontmen, one mic stand

Tsuneta and Iguchi split lead vocals within the same song rather than sticking to one "main singer" — a big part of King Gnu's signature sound.

02

Conservatory-trained

Tsuneta and Iguchi met as students at the Tokyo University of the Arts, which is where the band's jazz-and-classical backbone comes from.

03

Named after an animal, on purpose

"Gnu" (wildebeest) was chosen because the animal migrates in vast mixed herds — Tsuneta wanted the band's audience to grow the same way.

04

Written in four days

Their breakout hit "Hakujitsu" was composed and recorded on a tight drama-deadline turnaround — normally Tsuneta sends demos ahead, but this one was built live in studio.

05

Side project: Millennium Parade

Tsuneta also leads the audiovisual collective Millennium Parade, which shares members and a similarly genre-blurring approach.
